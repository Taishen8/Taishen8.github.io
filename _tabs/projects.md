This page collects the three projects that best explain my research trajectory. Each project page is organized around the same structure: the scientific problem, my contribution, the workflow, the main lesson, and selected visual evidence.

## SAPIENS

**AI-assisted discovery of catalytic self-assembling peptides.**

**Status:** ChemRxiv preprint; submitted to *JACS* after substantial revision informed by external peer review at *Nature Catalysis*.

This is the strongest bridge between my current work and my PhD direction. It connects LLM-assisted literature mining, dataset construction, positive-unlabeled learning, multi-task modeling, candidate prioritization, and experimental validation.

[Open SAPIENS](/projects/sapiens/)

## JCIM Peptide Inhibitor Design

**Model-guided screening of peptide inhibitors for the Hsp90 N-terminal domain.**

**Status:** Published in *Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling* (2024 JIF 5.3).

This project frames molecular design as a decision problem. Prediction scores, sequence mutations, docking evidence, and mechanistic interpretation all need to be considered before selecting candidates.

[Open JCIM](/projects/jcim/)

## ChemBART

**Pretraining and acceleration of a reaction-language model for synthesis planning.**

**Status:** Manuscript under review at *Nature Communications* (2024 JIF 15.7).

This project explains my experience with chemical foundation models, large-scale training bottlenecks, and practical engineering for AI-for-chemistry systems.

[Open ChemBART](/projects/chembart/)

## Connecting Theme

Together, these projects motivate my interest in evidence-grounded scientific agents: systems that can reason over uncertain literature, imperfect models, computational tools, synthesis constraints, and delayed experimental feedback.
