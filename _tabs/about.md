I am trained in computer science and chemistry, and I now focus on AI for scientific discovery. I am less interested in building isolated predictors and more interested in building discovery workflows that can decide under uncertainty and produce candidates worth testing.

My work so far follows a clear evolution:

1. **JCIM peptide inhibitor design** taught me how model scores, sequence mutations, docking evidence, and mechanism need to be considered together.
2. **ChemBART** taught me that scientific AI tools depend heavily on data pipelines, training systems, and practical engineering.
3. **SAPIENS** taught me how literature mining, dataset construction, model transfer, candidate prioritization, and validation can be connected into a discovery workflow.

For my PhD, I want to work on evidence-grounded and feedback-driven agents for molecular and materials discovery, especially in settings where closed-loop validation is realistic.
